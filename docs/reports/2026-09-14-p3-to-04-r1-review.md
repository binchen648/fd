## Prior Blocker 1 — Fixed-Duration Scheduler Boundary

**Closed.**

r1 introduces a normalized `ResolvedExpiryBoundary` with:

```text
kind=accepted_round_scheduler_boundary
owner=round_controller
boundaryPolicyId=<reviewed semantic scheduler boundary>
orderingPolicyId=<reviewed ordering policy>
```

`round_count` and `this_round` require `expiresOn`. The contract now separates:

1. round-counting convention; and
2. the authoritative scheduler boundary/order at which expiry settles.

Expiry is causally linked to the authoritative scheduler-boundary transition identity. Duplicate delivery cannot expire the same lifecycle twice. Phase polling, source-file order, card-specific conditionals, and card/ability-ID routing are forbidden substitutes.

The current `round_count=2` consumer remains runtime-blocked until its exact counting convention and scheduler boundary/order are independently confirmed. There is no implicit default.

## Fixed Duration / Cleanup Review

Accepted contract properties:

- positive finite duration required;
- no implicit install-round convention;
- no implicit expiry scheduler boundary/order;
- movement-producing expiry requires a resolved cleanup destination before runtime admission;
- state-only expiry is permitted only where the accepted policy explicitly requires no source movement;
- `remain_active` means no automatic Lifecycle-owned cleanup movement, not immunity from other accepted close/remove/transform rules;
- expiry + required external movement + lifecycle teardown commit atomically or roll back together.

## Reconnect / Idempotency / Failure Review

The contract defines stable server-owned lifecycle/transition identity and requires replay protection for:

- install;
- usage consumption;
- unique claim;
- expiry;
- source invalidation cleanup;
- reset/round-boundary processing.

Reconnect does not reinstall state, reset usage, reclaim a unique group, or repeat expiry. A failing dispatch preserves counters, claims, lifecycle records, cards, modifiers, external state, logs/history, and revision unless a prior command was already independently committed.
